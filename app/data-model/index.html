How the analysis works

No formulas needed. This is the whole idea in five steps, then one real example.

Put two numbers side by side

What the customer pays us for a location, and what we pay the vendor to clean it. Today these live in different systems and nobody compares them site by site.

Work out a fair cost

From the size of the site, how often it is cleaned, and what vendors in that city normally charge per hour, we estimate what the job should cost. Add our target margin and that gives a fair price.

Explain the difference

If a site makes less than it should, the shortfall is split across six reasons: the price is too low, the vendor charges too much, extra visits are not billed, the vendor travels too far, crews do work beyond the contract, or poor quality leads to refunds. The six always add up to the full shortfall.

Compare with sites that make money

Each site is set against profitable sites of the same kind, so we can see what the good ones do differently.

Look ahead and recommend

A forecast flags sites about to tip into loss. Then the Peazyyy AI app turns all of this into ranked fixes and automated actions.

What information it needs

From Peazy

  • Where each location is
  • Which vendor cleans it
  • Visits scheduled each week
  • Check-in and check-out times
  • Inspection scores and issues

From finance

  • What each customer is billed
  • Refunds given to customers
  • What each vendor is paid, including travel and extras

From contracts

  • How each customer is priced
  • When the contract started
  • Whether the price rises each year
  • Size of each site and what the cleaning covers

What it cannot tell you yet

  • The data here is made up to look like a real portfolio, so the figures show the method working, not the real picture.
  • "What vendors normally charge" is taken from what we pay today. If we overpay everyone in a city, it will look normal until outside wage data is added.
  • Route savings assume the recommended vendor can take on the extra sites.
